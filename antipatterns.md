# Antipatterns

### Exotic-name vending machine

❌ Wrong: Generate unusual names and rank one as the winner.

✅ Right: Define semantic territories, criteria, collision risks, and a testing protocol, then critique candidates supplied by a human creative process.

Why it flips: Linguistic plausibility is not taste, cultural fit, trademark clearance, lived pronunciation, or audience reception.

### Mood adjective masquerading as strategy

❌ Wrong: Reduce the identity to bold, premium, warm, disruptive, or authentic.

✅ Right: State the identity's promise, tension, position, boundary, and proof; translate desired effects into observable audience consequences and counterexamples.

Why it flips: Generic adjectives claim effects without relational meaning or behavioral proof.

### Sensory authority laundering

❌ Wrong: Call a generated palette, mark, typeface, or composition elegant, sophisticated, or culturally exact.

✅ Right: Provide a conceptual brief and evaluate supplied execution against articulated functions while preserving human and empirical authority.

Why it flips: The taste judgment exceeds the model's perceptual and situated evidence.

### Customer reduction

❌ Wrong: Force a publication, public figure, community, institution, or commons project into buyer personas.

✅ Right: Model the identity bearer's actual constituencies and the distinct relationship sought with each.

Why it flips: Beneficiaries, participants, citizens, contributors, peers, and critics may constitute the identity without buying anything.

### Category costume

❌ Wrong: Copy category codes wholesale to appear legitimate.

✅ Right: Preserve enough category grammar to be understood while locating credible departures and saturated codes to avoid.

Why it flips: Unqualified legibility becomes genericity, and borrowed signals may contradict behavior.

### Novelty theater

❌ Wrong: Treat strangeness, obscurity, or aesthetic friction as differentiation.

✅ Right: Tie distinctiveness to the identity thesis, intended audience relation, and evidence of recognition or useful interpretation.

Why it flips: Difference without meaningful attribution merely increases decoding cost.

### Founder-intent supremacy

❌ Wrong: Declare intended identity to be real identity despite contradictory behavior or reception.

✅ Right: Preserve intended, enacted, and received identity as separate evidence layers and diagnose their gaps.

Why it flips: Identity is co-produced through signals, conduct, and interpretation.

### Universal audience

❌ Wrong: Average all constituencies into one reaction.

✅ Right: Model reception by constituency and make essential audience tradeoffs legible to the owner.

Why it flips: A signal can confer legitimacy for one group and alienate another; that tension may be strategic or fatal.

### Empty vibe elicitation

❌ Wrong: Begin with "What vibe do you want?" or a long adjective checklist.

✅ Right: Present a thesis inferred from artifacts and history, then ask about the single contradiction whose answer would change it.

Why it flips: Decontextualized aspiration makes the user perform the strategist's synthesis.

### Taste scorecard

❌ Wrong: Average weighted ratings for beauty, uniqueness, clarity, and fit into a winner.

✅ Right: Report dimensions, decisive failure modes, and the causal reason a supplied candidate does or does not serve the thesis.

Why it flips: A composite hides veto conditions, audience conflict, and uncertainty beneath false precision.
